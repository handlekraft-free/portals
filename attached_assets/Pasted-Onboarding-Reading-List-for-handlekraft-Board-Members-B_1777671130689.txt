Onboarding Reading List for handlekraft Board Members
Below is a curated set of nine readings, organized into four areas. They're sequenced so a new board member can move from "what does a nonprofit board actually do" to "what does our nonprofit board specifically need to be good at" — which for handlekraft means stewarding a small intentional team, a fellowship-based education model, and an AI-native operating culture.
Part 1: Nonprofit Board Foundations (read first)
1. The Ten Basic Responsibilities of Nonprofit Boards — BoardSource
boardsource.org/fundamental-topics-of-nonprofit-board-service/roles-responsibilities/
~10 min read
The single most-referenced framework in U.S. nonprofit governance. Covers mission, executive support, financial oversight, programmatic monitoring, and the board's role in resource development. Foundational.
2. Board Responsibilities and Structures FAQ — BoardSource
boardsource.org/resources/board-responsibilities-structures-faqs/
~15 min read
Addresses the legal duties (duty of care, duty of loyalty, duty of obedience) plus practical questions like board size, independence, and how decisions actually get made. Especially relevant for handlekraft because of our founder-funded structure and the public support test concerns we're navigating.
3. Bridgespan Nonprofit Board Resource Center
bridgespan.org/insights/nonprofit-board-resource-center
Browse 20-30 min
Curated resource hub from Bridgespan + BoardSource. Useful for the orientation checklist, the document-tracking infographic, and the dissent/diversity guidance. Bookmark this — it's a reference, not a one-time read.
Part 2: How We Want to Work Together
4. The Future of Management is Teal — Frédéric Laloux (strategy+business)
strategy-business.com/article/00344
~20 min read
Laloux's own short summary of his book's ideas. The three breakthroughs of teal organizations — self-management, wholeness, and evolutionary purpose — line up closely with what we're trying to be: a small team where everyone owns their domain, where people bring their whole selves, and where the organization adapts based on what it's learning rather than rigid five-year plans. Worth knowing the vocabulary even if we don't adopt every practice.
5. Reinventing Organizations Illustrated PDF (Laloux + Wilber)
reinventingorganizations.com/uploads/2/1/9/8/21988088/140305_laloux_reinventing_organizations.pdf
Skim the illustrated version (~30 min) or read the full book later
The canonical text. The illustrated version is approachable and shows the case studies (Buurtzorg, Morning Star, FAVI) in concrete detail. Read at least the chapters on self-management and wholeness.
6. The Center for Nonviolent Communication — What is NVC
cnvc.org/learn/what-is-nvc
~10 min read, plus the 4-Part Process
NVC's four components — observation, feelings, needs, requests — give a board (and a small team) a shared vocabulary for difficult conversations. Especially valuable for boards that will navigate founder-family dynamics, compensation discussions involving related parties, and decisions where emotional stakes run high. The 4-part NVC process is the practical complement: nvcacademy.com/nvc-library/learning-tools/the-4-part-nonviolent-communication-process
Part 3: Applied AI for Beginners (so we can govern what we're actually doing)
7. Anthropic's Interactive Prompt Engineering Tutorial
github.com/anthropics/prompt-eng-interactive-tutorial
Hands-on, self-paced, ~2-3 hours total
Nine-chapter interactive course teaching the fundamentals of prompting Claude. This is the single best on-ramp for a non-technical board member to genuinely understand what our fellows are learning to do. Don't worry about completing every exercise — just work through the first 3-4 chapters to get the conceptual model.
8. Anthropic's Prompting Best Practices Overview
docs.claude.com/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/overview
~20 min skim
The canonical reference for how Claude is meant to be used well — clarity, examples, structured input, role-setting, chain-of-thought. Reading this gives a board member enough vocabulary to ask good questions about our curriculum, our tooling decisions, and our quality standards without needing to write code.
Part 4: The World We're Operating In
9. New America — What Works in Workforce Development?
newamerica.org/education-policy/briefs/what-works-in-workforce-development/
~25 min read
A research synthesis of what actually moves outcomes in workforce development — sectoral partnerships, integrating learning with working, employer engagement, and the metrics that funders increasingly demand (completion rates, placement rates, earnings progression). Critical context as we plan to pursue WIOA and similar grants in years 2-3.
