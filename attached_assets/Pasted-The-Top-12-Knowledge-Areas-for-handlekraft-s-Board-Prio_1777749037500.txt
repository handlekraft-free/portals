The Top 12 Knowledge Areas for handlekraft's Board (Priority Order)Tier 1: Critical for Survival and Compliance (Recruit First)1. Nonprofit Finance & Treasurer SkillsThis is your single most important board recruitment because handlekraft has an unusual financial profile that needs sophisticated oversight: heavy founder concentration, public support test management, DAF-routed contributions, and a 5-year diversification plan. You need a board treasurer who can read a 990, understand FASB nonprofit accounting standards, and translate financial statements for the rest of the board. Look for: nonprofit CFO, nonprofit auditor (CPA), or finance professional who has served on at least one nonprofit board before. This person should chair the Finance Committee and probably be your Treasurer.2. Nonprofit Governance & Legal KnowledgeSomeone who knows how nonprofit boards actually work — fiduciary duties, board roles, the IRS rules around disqualified persons and self-dealing, the rebuttable presumption procedures, and the practical mechanics of running effective board meetings. Critical given your founder/son insider transaction. Look for: experienced nonprofit board chair or vice chair, nonprofit attorney, or someone who has held senior nonprofit operations roles. This person likely becomes your Board Chair or Governance Committee Chair.3. Workforce Development & Education Policyhandlekraft sits at the intersection of education and workforce development, and you've explicitly identified workforce grants as a major future funding stream. You need someone with deep domain knowledge here — both to inform program design and to open doors when you become eligible for WIOA-funded work, state workforce board contracts, and similar opportunities. Look for: former community college dean of workforce development, ex-staff at a workforce investment board, sectoral training program leader, or workforce funder. This person becomes your bridge to the funding ecosystem you're targeting.Tier 2: Essential for Mission and Growth (Recruit Soon)4. Lived Experience as a Non-Traditional Learnerhandlekraft serves non-traditional learners — career changers, GED holders, single parents, veterans, self-taught individuals. The board must include voice and perspective from people who've actually walked this path. Without it, you risk designing programs that work in theory but miss in practice, and you lose credibility with funders who increasingly demand authentic lived experience on boards. Look for: someone who came into tech (or any professional career) through a non-traditional path, who can speak to barriers honestly. This person should sit on program-related committees.5. Fundraising & Major GiftsDistinct from Treasurer skills — this is the person who knows how to actually raise the money, not just manage it. Once you're past your own seed donations, you need active fundraising leadership. Look for: former development director, major gifts officer, or someone who has personally led a successful capital campaign. This person should chair a Development Committee (when you create one) and personally cultivate prospects.6. AI Ethics & Responsible Technologyhandlekraft is uniquely AI-native — your curriculum is "build tools using AI agents." The board needs at least one person who understands the broader context: AI safety considerations, bias issues, the labor economics of AI displacement, and how to credibly position handlekraft's work in this landscape. This is also a defensive recruitment — funders, journalists, and partners will have hard questions about teaching AI development in 2026 and beyond. Look for: AI ethics researcher, technology policy professional, or thoughtful technology executive who has wrestled with these questions in their own work.Tier 3: Important for Specific Functions (Recruit as Capacity Allows)7. Software Development & Engineering LeadershipWhile your son leads tools development operationally, the board needs someone who can credibly review the technical strategy, ask hard questions about quality and security, and provide a sounding board for the Tools and Development Lead that isn't his parent. This person can also help vet client engagements and assess product liability risk. Look for: senior engineering leader, CTO, or principal engineer who has built and shipped real products and can mentor without micromanaging.8. Marketing, Communications & StorytellingMission-driven nonprofits live and die on the quality of their storytelling. Your Marketing and Social Media Lead needs sophisticated direction, and the board itself needs someone who can think strategically about brand positioning, donor communications, and how to credibly differentiate handlekraft from the many tech-training nonprofits that exist. Look for: nonprofit communications director, marketing executive at a values-driven company, or experienced storyteller (journalist, documentarian) who has worked with social-impact organizations.9. Community Organization Leadership (Client Voice)handlekraft's clients are nonprofits and community organizations. You need a board member who runs one — someone who knows what it's like to need a website but not be able to afford one, who can validate that your services actually meet client needs, and who brings authentic community organization perspective. Look for: executive director of a small-to-medium community-based organization, ideally one that fits your target client profile. This person also helps with referrals and credibility in the sector.Tier 4: Strengthens Specific Capabilities (Add When Right Person Found)10. Human Resources & Organizational DevelopmentOnce you grow beyond two employees — and especially as you formalize a fellowship program with cohort intake, evaluation, and graduation — HR expertise becomes meaningful. This person can also help you think about culture deliberately as you scale, including the self-managing teal aspirations from your strategy document. Look for: nonprofit HR leader or organizational development consultant who has worked with small mission-driven teams.11. Diversity, Equity & Inclusion StrategyAuthentic DEI work is more than representation — it's about how access, opportunity, and power are distributed in the organization. handlekraft's mission demands genuine engagement here, and funders increasingly require it. This may overlap with your lived-experience director (#4) but ideally is a separate competency: someone who has led DEI strategy professionally and can hold the organization accountable to its stated values. Look for: DEI practitioner, equity-focused organizational consultant, or leader of an equity-centered organization.12. Government Relations & Public Sector NavigationIf workforce development funding becomes a meaningful revenue stream, you'll be navigating WIOA regulations, state workforce boards, county education partnerships, and federal grant compliance. Having someone who understands how government funding actually works — the proposal cycles, the reporting requirements, the political dynamics — is invaluable. Look for: former public sector workforce/education official, government affairs professional, or leader who has managed government grants at another nonprofit.
